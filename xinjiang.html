Destination Overview

Xinjiang — Where the Silk Road Lives

Xinjiang is China's largest and most diverse region — a land of staggering contrasts where the Taklamakan Desert meets snow-capped 7,000m peaks, where 2,000-year-old Silk Road oases are still inhabited, and where Uyghur, Kazakh, Tajik, and Tuva minority cultures maintain ancient ways of life. This is China's last great frontier: the Karakoram Highway winds to the Pakistani border, Kanas Lake hides in primeval alpine forest, and Kashgar's old town has barely changed since the days of Marco Polo. BWC makes this extraordinary region accessible and deeply rewarding.

Day by Day

Your 7-Day Xinjiang Itinerary

01
Day One
Arrival in Urumqi — Night Market & Uyghur Cuisine Dinner
Your BWC companion meets you at Urumqi Diwopu International Airport — the most inland city in the world, 2,500km from the nearest ocean. After settling into your hotel, the evening begins at the legendary Er Dao Qiao night market in the heart of Urumqi's Uyghur old quarter: a maze of stalls blazing with flatbreads fresh from tandoor ovens, whole roasted lamb on spits, towers of dried fruits and nuts, pomegranate juice, and the intoxicating fragrance of cumin and saffron. Your companion introduces you to Uyghur cuisine at a celebrated local restaurant — polo (fragrant lamb rice), lagman noodles hand-pulled at the table, and samsa pastries golden from the oven.
ArrivalNight MarketUyghur Cuisine
02
Day Two
Heavenly Lake (Tianchi) — Crater Lake, Kazakh Yurt & Horse Riding
Drive two hours into the Tianshan mountains to Tianchi (Heavenly Lake) — a stunning crater lake at 1,980m elevation, its sapphire waters ringed by snow-capped peaks and alpine meadows. A Kazakh nomad family hosts you in their traditional yurt — a circular felt dwelling used by the Kazakh people for centuries of pastoral life on these high grasslands. Your companion facilitates a genuine cultural exchange as you share kumiss (fermented mare's milk), dried meat, and flatbread with the family. Afternoon: mount Kazakh horses and ride through the mountain meadows with views of the lake and the Bogda Peak (5,445m) above.
TianchiYurtHorse Riding
03
Day Three
Ancient Silk Road — Jiaohe Ruins, Flaming Mountains & Grape Valley
Fly or drive to Turpan — one of the great Silk Road oases and the lowest, hottest point in China. Begin at Jiaohe Ruins (Yarkhoto): a 2,000-year-old ghost city built on a loess plateau between two rivers, once a thriving Buddhist metropolis of 6,500 people, now an eerie canyon of eroded mud-brick walls and temple towers stretching into the desert. Your companion brings the Silk Road merchants and monks of ancient Chang'an to life. Next: the Flaming Mountains — the blood-red sandstone range described in Journey to the West, radiating heat shimmer in the midday sun. Afternoon cooldown: Grape Valley, where Turpan's famous Muscat grapes ripen in trellised vineyards under the desert sun.
RuinsSilk RoadHistory
04
Day Four
Kashgar — Id Kah Mosque, Old Town & Sunday Livestock Market
Fly to Kashgar — the most remote major city on the old Silk Road, 4,000km from Beijing and closer to Baghdad than to Shanghai. Begin at the magnificent Id Kah Mosque — the largest mosque in China, its yellow-tiled facade presiding over a central square where 10,000 worshippers gather for Friday prayer. Your companion guides you through Kashgar Old Town: a living, breathing medieval Islamic city of mud-brick houses, carved wooden doors, copper workshops, and silk merchants — the same streets walked by Ibn Battuta, Marco Polo, and Xuanzang. The climax: the Sunday Livestock Market, billed as the world's largest — thousands of sheep, horses, camels, and cattle traded in clouds of dust while Uyghur farmers negotiate in Turkic languages.
MosqueOld TownMarket
05
Day Five
Karakoram Highway — Karakul Lake & Tajik Village Stay
Drive the legendary Karakoram Highway — one of the world's highest international roads, climbing from Kashgar toward the 4,693m Khunjerab Pass on the Pakistani border. The scenery is almost incomprehensibly dramatic: the highway winds through red desert canyons before emerging onto high alpine plateau. The destination is Karakul Lake — a gunmetal-blue glacial lake at 3,600m elevation, perfectly reflecting the 7,546m Muztagh Ata (Father of Ice Mountains) on its eastern shore. One of the most dramatic landscapes in Asia. Your BWC companion arranges a night in a Tajik village — the Tajik people, an Iranian people of ancient Silk Road origin, maintaining their distinctive embroidered costume, music, and hospitality traditions.
HighwayMountain LakeAdventure
06
Day Six
Kanas Lake — Pristine Alpine Scenery, Tuva Villages & Hemu Trek
Fly north to the Altai Mountains — Xinjiang's most pristine wilderness. Kanas Lake is a 25km-long glacial lake in primeval Siberian forest: its waters shift between emerald, turquoise, and deep blue with the season and cloud. The lake is legendary for its "Kanas Monster" — a mysterious large creature reputedly sighted in its depths. Your BWC companion leads you through the Tuva minority village of Hemu — wooden-log houses, log fences, and grazing horses in meadows of wild flowers that look like something from the Bavarian Alps or Swiss countryside, yet are entirely in Chinese northwest. Trek through the forest to viewpoints above the lake for photography at its finest.
KanasAlpineTrekking
07
Day Seven
Farewell Uyghur Breakfast & Return to Urumqi
Your final morning begins with a traditional Uyghur breakfast — freshly baked nan bread pulled from a tandoor oven, creamy sheep's milk yoghurt with honey, dried apricots, walnuts and raisins from Turpan, and fragrant black tea. Your BWC companion sits with you one last time, sharing reflections on seven extraordinary days across one of the world's most remarkable regions. Return flight to Urumqi for your international connection. Your companion accompanies you to the airport, ensures all luggage and documentation is in order, and bids you farewell — a journey you will never forget.
